Digital title · lifecycle

From paper to a unique, transferable digital title

The traditional paper document is transformed into a Verifiable Digital Title through a deterministic, five-step lifecycle: Create · Sign · Wrap · Mint · Send. Each step is executed automatically and cryptographically verifiable end-to-end.

During the Wrap stage, OPENmaat based on TradeTrust generates a Merkle proof that binds every data field of the document to a single tamper-evident hash. The Mint stage then registers the document on-chain as a transferable record, producing a unique token whose ownership is governed by two roles — the Beneficiary and the Holder.

Both roles are managed under ROX Custody MPC architecture, meaning that private keys are never exposed and every transfer or burn operation is signed by threshold cryptography. Only the connected owner can execute a change of custody, ensuring true singularity and control as required by MLETR.

  • Merkle-proof integrity — any modification invalidates the record.
  • On-chain title transfer — endorsements executed as verifiable transactions.
  • MPC custody — no single point of failure, no exposed private keys.
  • Portable delivery — export as JSON or PDF for legal, banking and customs use.

Standardization layer

Aligning trade data with DCSA standards

The Digital Container Shipping Association (DCSA) defines the global data standards used by the world's largest container carriers. It unifies the way booking, transport, tracking and electronic bills of lading are represented and exchanged across the maritime supply chain.

OPENmaat is being aligned with the DCSA information model so that every operation — from the initial booking to the final delivery — can be expressed with the same schema used by carriers, ports, banks and customs authorities. This transforms OPENmaat into a plug-in interoperability layer, rather than an isolated platform.

The result is a frictionless integration path: carriers can consume OPENmaat's eBL data without custom adapters, banks can validate documents against a common standard, and customs can rely on a predictable, machine-readable format.

  • Standardized eBL — DCSA-compliant electronic bill of lading structure.
  • Booking & Track — unified schema for shipment lifecycle events.
  • Universal API — a single connector for the entire trade ecosystem.
  • Carrier interoperability — direct compatibility with global shipping lines.

Financing layer

The Wrapp Model based on DLPC

An electronic Bill of Lading, when minted as a unique NFT, satisfies MLETR's requirement of singularity and control — but it also limits its financial utility, because a single indivisible token cannot be split, pooled or discounted across multiple investors.

The Wrapp Model solves this by encapsulating the underlying NFT title into a smart contract that issues fungible tokens (FT) representing fractional economic rights over the same asset. The title remains unique on-chain, while the value it represents becomes portable, divisible and financeable.

This is the foundation of DLPC — Digital Ledger Payable Commitment — a financing model that turns each verified trade document into a source of on-chain liquidity: invoice discounting, factoring, supply-chain finance and secondary markets, all built on top of the same MLETR-compliant title.

  • NFT preserves title — MLETR singularity remains intact.
  • Fungible layer for finance — economic rights become divisible and tradable.
  • DLPC-ready — a native path to on-chain trade finance.
  • Compatible with existing MLETR framework — no legal re-engineering needed.
